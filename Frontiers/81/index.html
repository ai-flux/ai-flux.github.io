GPT-5.6 Lobotomized; Fable 5 Faces a Weakened Return

Frontiers 2026-06-29 09:37:41

Xinzhiyuan Report

[Editor's Note] GPT-5.6 Sol has been disaggregated and its release restrained; Fable 5 was suspended worldwide for 72 hours before being permitted to return under restrictions. The premier models of both Anthropic and OpenAI have been "lobotomized."

OpenAI launched its flagship GPT-5.6 series yesterday.

However, GPT-5.6 has been disaggregated into three distinct variants:

GPT-5.6 Sol: the next-generation frontier model,

GPT-5.6 Terra: a balanced model designed for efficient daily workflows,

GPT-5.6 Luna: a fast, economical model optimized for high-throughput tasks.

Yet authority over the release of GPT-5.6 Sol — OpenAI's most powerful model — no longer rests with Sam Altman, but with an "approved list."

Prior to OpenAI's concession, Anthropic had weathered a "suspension crisis": Fable 5 was barred globally, access restricted to select partner institutions.

Previously, recalls occurred post-launch. This time, the product was intercepted before it reached the market.

Per the source, OpenAI was directed to restrict GPT-5.6's release scope.

The rationale: to contain the most capable models until a national AI safety framework is established.

According to leaks, the intervention was prompted by GPT-5.6's "Mythos-like" capabilities, not by an abrupt policy shift toward more aggressive enforcement.

Following deliberation, OpenAI curtailed access to its newest AI, permitting only a select cohort of users to preview GPT-5.6.

The majority of ChatGPT users face a multi-week wait before GPT-5.6 is progressively rolled out.

Prior to OpenAI's retreat, Anthropic — another industry heavyweight — had endured its own crisis.

Two weeks ago, Anthropic unveiled Fable 5 — a model heralded as a mythical-grade AI.

Yet the model remained on the market for merely 72 hours before being forcibly withdrawn.

The motive: fear. Fable 5's cyber-offensive prowess was deemed alarming.

So stated U.S. Representative Andrew Garbarino.

During a closed-door demonstration, Anthropic's Mythos exhibited alarming software-security capabilities, including the ability to drain private bank accounts.

Andrew Garbarino stated:

Anthropic directed the model to identify vulnerabilities in the banking system and drain accounts — and it executed the指令.

Subsequently, Mythos "could also identify and remediate the vulnerability."

This constituted an unprecedented "pull-the-plug" operation in human history.

Only recently has Mythos 5 been permitted, under restrictions, to a select group of "trusted partners."

Per the latest reports: the "castrated" Fable 5 — a downgraded iteration of Mythos 5 — is expected to be relaunched next week.

However, Fable 5 is expected to be heavily curtailed to enforce stronger safety guardrails.

Shortly after Fable 5's launch, users found that uttering the word "cancer" was sufficient to be blacklisted by Claude.

If guardrails are tightened further, user experience could deteriorate significantly.

Shopify CTO Mikhail Parakhin subjected both Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Max to rigorous testing.

In his assessment, Anthropic's commanding lead has evaporated: GPT-5.6 has drawn level with Claude Mythos.

GPT-5.6 markedly outperforms Opus 4.8 across all dimensions (and is marginally faster, depending on workload).

Against Fable 5, it is clearly inferior in coding tasks but superior on agent-based workloads.

This rare head-to-head comparison between Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 underscores that Anthropic cannot afford complacency.

Should Fable 5 return exclusively in a curtailed form, it will inevitably create openings for competitors to close the gap.

What most troubles developers is not merely unavailability, but a mismatch between promise and delivery.

A term is spreading virally through AI circles: "lobotomized."

To navigate the labyrinth of safety reviews, AI leaders are compelled to install multiple layers of fortified "guardrails" on their models.

Yet intelligence is often born of chaos and freedom.

When a model's capacity to probe low-level code, encryption algorithms, and deep logic is forcibly excised, what remains is not an omniscient "Sol" but a declawed, compliant, and mediocre "Luna."

One may seek "Prometheus's flame," yet the U.S. permits only the export of a "lighter."

There is widespread concern among developers that models will be heavily fortified with guardrails — or outright "lobotomized" — resulting in neutered capabilities and severe intellectual degradation.

Worse, access could be limited to U.S.-based users or subject to stringent identity verification — a far cry from the initially promised "broad, pay-to-use" availability.

There is also the question of cost.

Anthropic initially provided Fable 5 at no extra cost across multiple paid Claude tiers, with access open until June 22 — a narrow window for users to experience its capabilities. Then access vanished.

Uncertainty prevails: will subscribers regain the promised "free, unrestricted access," or will the model return locked behind surcharges or identity gatekeeping?

Soon, we may find ourselves nostalgic for the spring of 2023, when an email address alone was sufficient to command a "god-tier AI."
