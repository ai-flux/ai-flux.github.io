GPT-4 Family Fully Retired by OpenAI in Four and a Half Months

Frontiers 2026-06-29 09:37:41

Reported by Synced

[Synced导读] The final GPT-4 model has been retired. Over four and a half months, OpenAI phased out its entire GPT-4 family, with GPT-4.5 as the last to depart. No formal farewell was given, only a single changelog entry — model retirements have become routine for the AI industry.

On June 26, GPT-4.5 was officially retired from ChatGPT; custom GPTs running on it were discontinued as well.

OpenAI cited continued retirement of underutilized legacy models to allocate capacity for newer, more capable alternatives.

The retirement had been telegraphed a month in advance.

On May 28, OpenAI announced a 30-day sunset period for GPT-4.5. Once elapsed, the model would be removed from the model selector, and in-progress conversations would be migrated to GPT-5.5 automatically.

Nevertheless, retirement day saw a surge of nostalgic tributes across social media.

"Witnessing GPT-4.5's retirement is genuinely disheartening. May the legend rest in peace."

That was one sentiment expressed.

In his view, it was never merely a conventional model: in creative writing, it possessed taste, grasping tone, rhythm, and emotion, and handling even the subtle, idiosyncratic nuances of human expression.

"Smarter models will continue to emerge, but GPT-4.5 possessed a soul."

A user remarked bluntly that GPT-4.5 is the only model in the ChatGPT family whose departure is genuinely regretted.

Another user cataloged the departures: GPT-4o previously, GPT-4.5 now. Who would be next? In their assessment, these models were never mere tools.

Others observed that AI models now have shorter product lifespans than gym memberships.

GPT-4.5 was the final GPT-4 family model remaining in ChatGPT, marking the complete elimination of the GPT-4 line from the product.

On February 13, GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1 mini, and o4-mini were retired in a single wave.

On March 11, GPT-5.1 was retired.

On June 12, the full GPT-5.2 series was retired.

In late June, GPT-4.5 was withdrawn.

From February through June — four and a half months — OpenAI retired the entire GPT-4 family and several 5.x-series iterations.

The once-viral Ghibli-style GPT-4o — likened to Samantha from the film Her — has long since vanished from ChatGPT's model menu.

August 26 will mark o3's retirement.

GPT-4.5 was among OpenAI's largest and most costly foundational models, positioned at launch for nuanced writing and dialogue. Owing to its weight and latency, access was restricted to paid users only.

The impact extended beyond the chat interface.

Custom GPTs built on GPT-4.5 were required to change their underlying model as of that date. Enterprise teams with workflows dependent on 4.5 faced two options: migrate to GPT-5.5 or finalize their work within the grace period.

A few years ago, the world waited nearly three years for the transition from GPT-3 to GPT-4. The leap from GPT-4 to GPT-5 took over a year.

Today, a flagship model version frequently exits ChatGPT within six months of its debut.

GPT-5.1 remained in ChatGPT for four months; GPT-5.2 for under six. Many users were unaware they had interacted with it before its name was removed from the menu.

This trajectory is driven by industrial logic.

Computing costs continue to decline. Newer models render their predecessors immediately obsolete on benchmarks upon release. With switching costs near zero for users, legacy models retain no rationale for remaining in the menu.

This cadence is not unique to OpenAI. Google and Anthropic are competing on the same track, and neither can afford to decelerate.

When Anthropic removed Sonnet 4.5 from the Claude app this May, that version had a lifespan of only eight months.

Anthropic's official model lifecycle table shows Sonnet 4.5 remains Active at the API layer, with a scheduled retirement date of September 29, 2026 at the earliest — yet it was withdrawn from claude.ai's model selector on May 15.

Rapid model iteration is, on the surface, beneficial to users — a smarter model is almost always available.

But there is a harsher reality: a model you have grown accustomed to can vanish without notice.

Although GPT-4.5 has been removed from the ChatGPT product, it has not been deleted — declaring the GPT-4 era fully concluded would be imprecise.

In the May 28 changelog that announced the retirement, OpenAI stated explicitly that the retirement applies only to ChatGPT and does not affect the API.

In other words, developers retain API access to GPT-4.5 without modifying a single line of code in their workflows.

GPT-4.5's retirement operates on two distinct schedules for ChatGPT and the API. OpenAI guarantees a minimum six-month retirement notice for flagship API models. Even the frozen early versions of GPT-5 and o3 were only announced to developers on June 11, with full removal slated for year-end.

The same model can exhibit a 'time of death' discrepancy exceeding six months between the two product surfaces.

Thus, what 'vanished' for GPT-4.5 is merely the consumer-facing entry point. Model weights and capabilities remain intact — they are simply no longer selectable from the app's dropdown menu.

OpenAI even preserved a mechanism for enterprise and education users: enabling 'Legacy model access' in workspace settings allows certain older models to remain usable during the transition period.

Underlying these retirements is OpenAI's strategy of consolidating the user-facing model lineup from a parallel array of options onto the GPT-5.5 mainline.

Retiring legacy models shortens the menu selection. New users are no longer confronted with choice paralysis across a dozen options.

A more pragmatic consideration is compute capacity.

Each legacy model still listed in the menu requires allocated compute resources to remain on standby. Retiring low-usage versions frees capacity that can be diverted to newer models and growing demand volumes.

Retiring legacy models represents, in effect, a reallocation of compute resources.

Even as the mainline contracts on one front, new branches emerge on the other.

Per an OpenAI update, the next-generation GPT-5.6 has entered a limited preview phase.

This is the prevailing reality at leading AI labs: model retirements and launches have become a standard product cadence for industry leaders such as OpenAI and Anthropic.

Why did GPT-4.5 earn such widespread fondness among users?

On X and Reddit, GPT-4.5 was widely referred to as 'the best writing model' and 'the one with a soul.'

Although newer models surpass 4.5 by a wide margin in mathematics and coding, what users mourn is not benchmark performance.

What they miss is something intangible — the tone, the cadence, the sense of being 'met' in conversation.

This is not the first time OpenAI has confronted such user attachment.

When GPT-5 was released last August, GPT-4o was briefly removed. User backlash was severe enough that the company reversed its decision within days, reinstating the model for paying users only.

That wave of sentiment also spawned a #Keep campaign. Petition websites were created; calls were made for OpenAI to open-source the legacy model.

A petition page created by users seeking to preserve GPT-4o

GPT-4o was ultimately fully retired this February. That wave of feedback, however, shaped the 'personality' configurations of subsequent model generations, leading to the inclusion of adjustable attributes such as warmth and enthusiasm.

Moreover, users perceived distinct roles: GPT-4o served companionship, GPT-4.5 served writing — each occupied a distinct position.

GPT-5.5 is now smarter and more versatile — whether it can fill the void left by 4.5 in 'linguistic intuition' remains uncertain. This is not a problem that greater intelligence alone can solve.

An algorithmically generated artifact, and yet a community bids it a solemn farewell. A decade ago, such a scene would have been inconceivable. Today, it unfolds every few months.

From GPT-4o, to GPT-4.5, to o3 on August 26 — the roster of farewells continues to expand.

For over a billion AI users worldwide, the salient concern is not that 'another model has vanished' — it is that what was perceived as a stable tool has a shelf life often shorter than six months. The expiration date was affixed at launch.

And this cadence of model retirements — OpenAI, Anthropic, and their peers will not decelerate because of anyone's sentiment.
