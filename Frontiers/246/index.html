Apple's HomePod mini 2 Is Expected This Fall, Six Years After the Original, With the AI Chip Driving the Refresh

Nearly six years after its 2020 launch, the HomePod mini is finally due for an update. Reports now point to a second-generation model arriving as early as this fall; the extended gap has been attributed to Apple's wait for a mature next-generation Siri. Nor is the HomePod mini the only casualty: a new Apple TV 4K, the full-size HomePod, and smart-home displays have all been delayed by the pace of Apple's AI work. AI capability has thus emerged as the pivotal constraint on Apple's hardware cadence, with software readiness now dictating the timing of product releases.

The current HomePod mini is powered by the S5, a processor introduced with the 2019 Apple Watch Series 5 and responsible, in the speaker, for computational audio and environment-adaptive sound. The second-generation model is expected to adopt a more powerful part—previously rumored to be an S9 or higher. The latest report declines to name the exact chip, but confirms a faster processor, intended to satisfy the on-device AI demands of next-generation Siri. The cross-generational leap in compute is aimed squarely at enabling the compact speaker to genuinely comprehend and reason.

**UWB and Sound, Upgraded in Tandem**

On connectivity, the current model is equipped with a U1 chip, enabling interactions such as handoff in proximity to an iPhone. A shift to more advanced ultra-wideband hardware is expected to sharpen device recognition and response times, delivering more precise, seamless cross-device coordination. Better audio performance has also been reported for the HomePod mini 2, though changes to the drivers, microphone array, and acoustic architecture have yet to be disclosed.

For Apple, the HomePod mini 2 represents far more than a routine refresh of the speaker line. At a pivotal juncture in Siri's AI-driven reinvention, the entry-level device is positioned as a primary gateway to Apple's on-device AI experience. Whether the new chip can rehabilitate a voice assistant long regarded as the weakest among its peers will be decided this fall.
